Drugs@FDA: from a FAERS report to a Product and its label

Drugs@FDA is FDA's list of every approved application: NDA (new drug, usually a brand), ANDA (generic) and BLA (biologic). Each application has its products, strengths and sponsor. FAERS carries the application number in nda_num, so this is the bridge from a report to a Product, and from the Product to its label. Every number was measured on 2026-10-20 by prototypes/explore/identity_atlas.py on the 2026 Q1 + Q2 bulk FAERS files and the Drugs@FDA file of 2026-10-20.

Verdict: KEEP as the Product source. It reaches a Product for 72.8% of our suspect rows (82.6% with a brand-name fallback), but only 37–40% for opioids. And nda_num is not always the product the patient used: 40,597 Zepbound rows carry Mounjaro's number. Signals stay at Drug level; Product is a drill-down. Companion page: rxnorm.html (Drug level).

Brand fallback = blank nda_num, and the reported name equals a Drugs@FDA product name that belongs to exactly one application of the same ingredient (MOUNJARO 6,185 rows, ZEPBOUND 5,069, TRULICITY 798). It barely helps opioids: their blank rows say OXYCODONE (2,624 rows, no exact product name) or HYDROMORPHONE HYDROCHLORIDE (1,423 rows, 25 generic applications). Which generic maker is unknowable. * see the Zepbound problem below.

The number is not always the product the patient used
  • ZEPBOUND rows carry Mounjaro's number. 40,597 suspect rows named ZEPBOUND or ZEPBOUND KWIKPEN have nda_num 215866 (Mounjaro, diabetes). Zepbound has its own application, 217806 (weight loss). Across all ZEPBOUND rows: 40,610 say 215866, 1 says 217806, 5,853 are blank. Same ingredient, so Drug counts are fine. But the Product and the reference label would be wrong for 54% of all tirzepatide suspect rows.
  • Smaller cases of the same thing: VICTOZA rows on Saxenda's number (58), SUBUTEX on Suboxone's (13), NORSPAN on Butrans's (9).
  • Wrong application entirely: 64 rows. The number exists but holds a different ingredient: 050273 Achromycin (tetracycline) on 29 buprenorphine rows; 072169 ibuprofen on 31 oxycodone/tramadol rows. This is why we check that the application's ingredient matches.
Number format
  • FAERS drops leading zeros. Across all FAERS rows, 61,336 five-digit numbers match only after padding to 6 digits (559 still don't). Pad to 6.
  • 99 is a placeholder: 263 of our rows (tirzepatide 136, semaglutide 123). Other unknown numbers: 29038 on 43 oxycodone rows, 059175 on 39 hydrocodone rows, 38424 on 15 hydromorphone rows. Not in Drugs@FDA; cause unverified.
  • 387 rows carry a number Drugs@FDA doesn't know; 7 are longer than 6 digits (e.g. 20210014).
One brand, many applications; one label, many applications
  • The name OZEMPIC belongs to 2 applications: 209637 (injection) and 213051 (the tablet, also sold as Rybelsus). WEGOVY to 2: 215256 (injection) and 218316 (tablet, approved 2025-12-22). A name alone can't pick the product, and route is blank on 89% of semaglutide rows (walkthrough).
  • The Wegovy label ee06186f… lists both NDA215256 and NDA218316 in its <approval> blocks. Label ↔ application is many-to-many.
  • Drugs@FDA holds 664 applications for our 13. Hydrocodone alone has 205 (192 generics, 199 combinations, 81 sponsors). FAERS rows reach 241 of them.
openFDA's harmonised fields are empty where it matters
  • openfda.spl_set_id is filled for 11,885 of 29,390 applications, and for 161 of the 241 we reach. It is empty for Ozempic (NDA209637), Wegovy (NDA215256, NDA218316), Hysingla (NDA206627) and Actiq (NDA020747).
  • openFDA's label search by openfda.application_number:"NDA209637" returns 3 repackager labels and misses Novo's own label, whose openfda block is empty.

Two class-wide waves hit the opioids: 22 Dec 2025, 195 labeling approvals in one day (307 in the month across 230 applications), and 18 Jun 2026, 255 REMS approvals in one day. Decision to propose

Proposed
  • Product = one FDA application (ApplType + ApplNo, e.g. NDA209637). Not strength-level: FAERS can't reach it.
  • FAERS → Product rule: pad nda_num to 6 digits; accept only if the application's ingredient matches the row's RxNorm ingredient. 99, blank, unknown or mismatched → Product = unknown, Drug still known.
  • Brand beats number: if the reported name is a brand owned by exactly one application of the same ingredient, and nda_num points elsewhere, use the brand's application. Tag nda overridden. Fixes the 40,597 Zepbound rows.
  • Blank number: use the unique-brand fallback (+14,009 rows). Otherwise leave Product unknown. Don't guess a generic maker.
  • Signals stay at Drug level (D027). The Product view is a drill-down that shows "% of reports with a known product" (opioids ~40%, GLP-1 ~94%).
  • Application → label: read the application numbers from each label's <approval> block (the label pipeline parses the XML anyway); DailyMed's application_number search as a cross-check. Store as a many-to-many link. Reference label = the sponsor's own label, else the newest. Don't use openFDA's harmonised fields.
  • Ingest: reload the 6 MB zip in full each weekday (a snapshot, no incremental logic needed). Diff Submissions day to day to emit ChangeEvents, grouped by date + submission class.

What moves forward

  • Into the build: product (one row per application: type, number, sponsor, brand names, forms, marketed yes/no) · product_ingredient (application → IN RxCUI) · product_label (application ↔ setid, many-to-many, sponsor flag) · on each FAERS drug row: product_id + how it was set (nda / brand override / brand fallback / unknown).
  • Into monitoring: % of suspect rows with a known product, per ingredient per quarter · count of overridden numbers · count of unknown numbers (99 and others).
  • Into ChangeEvent: new application approved, labeling supplement approved, REMS change, product discontinued. Grouped per day and class.
  • Open questions: is the Zepbound → 215866 pattern a reporter convention we should ask FDA about? · a better reference-label rule for the 63 unclear cases · approval → DailyMed lag (labels session).
  • Your hand-check: open Drugs@FDA 215866 and 217806: Mounjaro and Zepbound are separate applications.
