SrLC: when a reaction was added to the label

SrLC (Drug Safety-related Labeling Changes) is FDA's database of approved safety edits to drug labels, from January 2016 onward. Each entry is one approved supplement: a date, the label sections it touched, and the changed text with additions underlined. It is the only source that says "this reaction joined the label on this date". This page measures what it holds for our 13 ingredients and whether it can support a backtest. Every number was measured on 2026-10-20 by prototypes/explore/label_atlas.py (steps srlc, srlcan) from the live SrLC web pages.

Verdict: KEEP, small Use SrLC for two things: a "safety change approved" ChangeEvent, and the dated history behind a backtest. Read the HTML pages, not the CSV. Backtest is feasible in design, but it needs older FAERS quarters (cut item, D031 v2). Companion pages: dailymed.html · onsides.html.

3

What's messy (measured)

Found
  • The name search matches inside words. "morphine" returns apomorphine; "hydrocodone" returns benzhydrocodone. This is the same trap we saw in FAERS. Filter on the exact ingredient after the search.
  • Combination products dominate some ingredients. Hydrocodone: 14 products, only 3 single-ingredient (cough syrups, ibuprofen and acetaminophen combinations).
  • The CSV is broken. The Ozempic CSV starts with a blank line. One field holds HTML with a stray quote (target=_blank"), which splits rows: only 31 rows parse with all 11 columns, and 37 fragments do not. Supplement 12, which is on the HTML page, is lost. The CSV also has no date column. Dates exist only on the HTML page.
  • Underlines mark edits, not reactions. Underlined pieces include whole sentences, headings ("Renal:"), single words ("acute", "or", "which may include") and trial descriptions. Turning them into reaction terms needs Part B's extractor or a before/after text diff.
  • "Added" is not always new. Necrotizing pancreatitis was underlined in §6.2 on 2026-06-01, but the same label already said "hemorrhagic or necrotizing pancreatitis" in §5.2 since 2025-01-28. Cholecystitis was underlined in 2022 and again in 2025. A backtest must use the first date the reaction appears anywhere in the 4 safety sections.
  • Edits arrive in bundles. SUPPL-35 and SUPPL-37 were approved the same day and reached DailyMed in one version (v17).
  • One odd lag. Hydromorphone's 2016-12-16 change maps to the next DailyMed version 726 days later. The label's history lists no version in between. Probably missing history, not a real 2-year lag (unverified).

Across the 101 products for our 13 ingredients there are 534 entries since 2016. They cluster: in 2025, 70 entries fell on only 9 approval dates, and 46 of them on one day (Dec 22, the class-wide opioid update). In 2026 so far: 13 entries on 8 dates. So a safety change for our drugs is a few events per year, and an opioid wave touches all 8 opioids at once.

6

Decision

Proposed
  • ChangeEvent "safety change approved": twice a week, run the date search for the last 30 days, keep rows whose exact ingredient is ours, and parse the detail page. Show it as "FDA approved a safety label change (sections 5, 6). DailyMed text usually follows in about 2 weeks." Then link the DailyMed version when it lands.
  • Parse the HTML detail page, never the CSV. Store each entry as (application, supplement, approval date, section, underlined text, full text).
  • Backtest (cut item): for each reaction added to a reference label, take its first-appearance date in the 4 sections. Then compute our signal on the FAERS quarters before that date. Feasible, but our local FAERS covers only 2026 Q1 + Q2, after every Ozempic addition except the June 2026 one (which was not new). A real backtest needs the 2023 to 2025 quarterly files: about 12 zips of about 60 MB each.
  • SrLC is not a label source. It holds edits, not the full label. DailyMed stays the label truth.
