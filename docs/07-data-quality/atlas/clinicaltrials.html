Do clinical trials back up the FAERS signals?

A 2-hour keep/park test. We pulled every ClinicalTrials.gov study with posted results for our 13 drugs, sorted out the ones that are not really about our drug, and looked for the top FAERS signals in their side-effect tables. Every number was measured on 2026-10-20 by prototypes/explore/ctgov_gate.py.

Verdict: KEEP-NARROW GLP-1 trials as a one-time snapshot (about 2 h) that adds "seen in trials" evidence to a signal. PARK opioid trials. Still first on the cut list (D031).

Get it

What it is

ClinicalTrials.gov is the US registry of clinical trials. When a sponsor posts results, the record includes an adverse-event table: side effects per arm (drug vs placebo), with how many people were at risk. FAERS has no such denominator.

How we pulled it
GET https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies
    ?query.intr=semaglutide          ← intervention search (expands synonyms, see part 3)
    &aggFilters=results:with         ← only studies with posted results
    &fields=protocolSection.identificationModule,…,resultsSection.adverseEventsModule
    &pageSize=200 &pageToken=…       ← page until no token

Public, no key, JSON. All 13 drugs: 13.1 seconds, 33 MB raw, 61,611 side-effect rows.

3

What's messy

What's messy (measured)
  • The search returns other drugs. 288 of 543 morphine hits (53%) never name morphine. The search expands to hydromorphone (69 studies), oxymorphone (14) and others. One hit was a multiple sclerosis trial; our guess is that "MS" was read as "morphine sulfate" (unverified).
  • Placebo arms carry the drug name. "Placebo (semaglutide)". Our first run took these as drug arms and put 43 semaglutide trials in "drug in every arm". After the fix: 8. A naïve name match hides most placebo-controlled trials.
  • Our drug is often background. 332 studies give the drug to every arm (for example, morphine as rescue pain relief in a ketamine trial). Another 121 are bioequivalence / PK / abuse-liability studies. 408 use our drug only as the comparator for a new drug.
  • Arms are named loosely. "Sema 2.4 mg", "Group A", "1". In 108 drug-tested studies, no side-effect group could be tied to our drug by name.
  • Vocabulary is free text. 201 different spellings of sourceVocabulary. Rows: 50,627 MedDRA (versions 2.0 to 29.0), 7,851 blank, 2,095 CTCAE, 202 COSTART, 396 odd (for example "NAUSEA" or "PLACEBO" typed into the vocabulary field).
  • Rare events can be hidden. 851 studies only list non-serious events seen in at least 5% of an arm. Serious events are always listed, which is why NAION shows up.
  • Counts are tiny. NAION appears 1 to 2 times per trial. No single trial can show a difference on its own.

Why oxycodone fails
  • Its top FAERS "signals" (rash, joint swelling, rheumatoid arthritis) come from co-medicated rheumatology patients, not from oxycodone. Trials cannot confirm a confounded signal. Pain trials list "Pain" at background rates (1 in 100–360).

6

Decision

Gate rule: 2 or fewer useful hits → PARK
  • PARK Opioids. 0 to 1 useful trial per oxycodone signal. Only 23% of opioid hits are about the drug's safety. High cleaning cost, near-zero signal value.
  • KEEP-NARROW GLP-1s. NAION: 5 useful trials. Gastric emptying: 8. The MedDRA terms match FAERS exactly. Scope: 5 GLP-1 drugs (489 studies, 0.5 s per drug), one-time snapshot, serious events only, and only terms that FAERS already flags. It shows as a "Seen in trials" line on the signal card, not as a new signal.
  • Estimated cost: about 2 hours. The script already has the arm rules. Export one CSV, upload it to Foundry, and add a TrialEvidence object linked to Signal. A live weekly pipeline would cost about 4 hours and is not worth it at 110% load. It stays first on the cut list. If cut, the note still says "explored: trials confirm NAION in 5 controlled trials".

🩺 PV SME view

Trials are a fair second opinion for a serious, rare event like NAION, because serious events are always listed. A handful of cases cannot prove causation. Show counts per trial; never show a pooled rate as if it were a real incidence.

💊 Safety officer view

"NAION: 394 FAERS reports; also seen in 5 controlled trials (8 on drug vs 1 on control), click for each trial." That line makes the flag easier to trust, and every trial links to its source.
