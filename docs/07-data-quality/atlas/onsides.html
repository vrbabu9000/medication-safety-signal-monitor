OnSIDES: a second opinion on Part B, with limits

OnSIDES is a public table of label side effects from the Tatonetti lab. A fine-tuned language model (PubMedBERT, a BERT model trained on medical text) scores every MedDRA term it finds in the Adverse Reactions, Boxed Warning and Warnings sections of each label. We planned to use it as a benchmark for our own extractor: not a pipeline source, not a backtest. This page measures whether it can play that role for our labels. Every number was measured on 2026-10-20 by prototypes/explore/label_atlas.py (step onsides) on the OnSIDES v3.1.1 data release.

Verdict: DOWNGRADE OnSIDES lacks 9 of our 13 reference labels, including Ozempic and OxyContin. Its stand-in copies are 2021 and 2022 versions. On the two gold-set drugs it lists 16 and 25 terms, against 44 and 147 in our draft. Keep it as an optional, cheap cross-check of precision, not as the second accuracy number. It is already #2 on the cut list. Companion pages: dailymed.html · srlc.html.

What's messy (measured)

Found
  • 82% of effect rows are repeats. 5,693,345 of 6,928,666 rows repeat a (label, term, section) already seen, with identical scores, in 39,347 labels. The Hysingla label has 1,848 rows for 30 unique terms. Any count must de-duplicate first.
  • Our brand reference labels are missing. Present: Hysingla (v13, Jun 2024; today v17), hydromorphone (v16, Jan 2024; today v20), tramadol Teva (v5, Jun 2025; today v6), methadone Ascend (v11, current). Missing: Brixadi, fentanyl patch, MS Contin, OxyContin, Ozempic, Victoza, Trulicity, Byetta, Mounjaro.
  • Stand-ins are old or wrong. Ozempic's closest copy is an A-S Medication repackager at v1 (Jan 2021). OxyContin has no copy; the closest is a generic oxycodone ER label at v4 (Jan 2022). For fentanyl, our "same brand word" rule picked a Hospira injection for a patch reference: wrong route, not usable. MS Contin's copy dates from 2010.
  • Section headings counted as reactions. On the oxycodone stand-in, "Cardiac disorder" and "Gastrointestinal disorder" come from the system headings ("Cardiac disorders:"), not from reactions.
  • Matches inside words. "TEN" (toxic epidermal necrolysis, a severe skin reaction) is listed in the Boxed Warning and W&P of that label. The letters exist only inside "EXTENDED" and "THREATENING". On Ozempic, "Diabetic" alone is listed as a reaction (from "diabetic retinopathy").
  • Rows with no section. 388,407 rows carry section "NA" instead of AR, BW or WP.
  • Mixed term levels. 31% of rows on both stand-ins are lowest-level terms ("Diarrhea", "Hypoglycemia", "Seizures") rather than the preferred terms FAERS uses ("Diarrhoea", "Hypoglycaemia", "Seizure"). Matching them needs the MedDRA hierarchy, which we do not have.

The oxycodone stand-in contains 148 of our 155 gold phrases, so the text is there. Yet OnSIDES lists only 25 terms for it. The long system-organ lists in §6.1 ("Psychiatric disorders: abnormal dreams, anxiety, confusion…") are mostly absent from OnSIDES. Read this as a lower bound: naming differences hide some true matches. It is still far from the 0.85 hold-out F1 (accuracy score) OnSIDES reports for Adverse Reactions.

6

Decision

Proposed
  • Our hand-made gold set is the only accuracy number for Part B (D015 target F1 ≥ 0.85). OnSIDES is not a second accuracy number for our labels.
  • Optional precision cross-check (cut #2): run our extractor on the 4 labels OnSIDES has at a known version (Hysingla v13, hydromorphone v16, tramadol v5, methadone v11), fetching those exact versions. Report where the two disagree. Do not score recall against OnSIDES.
  • Reuse their lessons, not their output: their sections (AR, BW, WP) match our 4 parent sections minus Contraindications. Their errors (headings, matches inside words, "Diabetic") go into our prompt's "do not extract" list and the grounding check (quote must be whole words).
  • Licensing: cite OnSIDES if we mention it; never ship its term lists; MedDRA question to the 🛡️ Compliance lens.
