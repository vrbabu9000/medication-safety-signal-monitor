RxNorm: one ID for each of our 13 drugs

RxNorm is the US National Library of Medicine's drug dictionary. It gives every drug concept one ID, the RxCUI. We use the ingredient-level ID as the key of the Drug object. This page measures how well it turns the ingredient text in real FAERS rows into our 13 IDs. Every number was measured on 2026-10-20 by prototypes/explore/identity_atlas.py on the 2026 Q1 + Q2 bulk FAERS files, against RxNorm release 05-Oct-2026.

Verdict: KEEP RxNorm exact lookup reaches the Drug for 171,166 of 171,171 rows that are really ours (99.997%). Adding one salt word ("trihydrate") gets the last 5. The FAERS ingredient field is already clean, so RxNorm's real value is a stable ID and the drug family graph, not better matching. Companion page: drugsatfda.html (Product level).

What one record looks like

RxNorm is a graph, not a table

One FAERS row: …$PS$OXYCONTIN$OXYCODONE HYDROCHLORIDE$…. The coded ingredient goes to RxNorm. These are the real responses:

rxcui.json?name=OXYCODONE HYDROCHLORIDE   → {"idGroup":{"rxnormId":["82063"]}}
rxcui/82063/properties.json               → {"rxcui":"82063","name":"oxycodone hydrochloride","tty":"PIN"}
rxcui/82063/related.json?tty=IN           → {"tty":"IN","conceptProperties":[{"rxcui":"7804","name":"oxycodone"}]}

tty is the concept type. IN = ingredient (our Drug key). PIN = precise ingredient, a salt form of an IN. Below them sit clinical drugs (SCD), branded drugs (SBD) and package codes (NDC).

What's messy (measured)

The coded field is clean; the edges are not
  • Only 41 distinct ingredient strings in prod_ai contain one of our 13 words, across 2 quarters. FDA codes this field. Compare the free-text drugname: 77 spellings of oxycodone alone in one quarter (walkthrough page).
  • 30 of 41 strings are in RxNorm by exact name. RxNorm already knows the salt forms (PIN), so stripping salt words found nothing extra with the walkthrough's salt list. One string was found only after also stripping "trihydrate": OXYCODONE HYDROCHLORIDE TRIHYDRATE, 5 rows. The walkthrough rule misses those 5 too.
  • 11 strings RxNorm does not know (43 rows): street analogues and metabolites. Parafluorofentanyl 15, nicomorphine 8, desmetramadol 6, norbuprenorphine 5, norfentanyl 2, 6-acetylmorphine 2, despropionylfentanyl 2, and 4 single rows. None of them is one of our drugs. Today they drop out as "unknown". They should drop out as "not ours, tagged".
  • Look-alikes RxNorm correctly separates (2,773 rows): apomorphine 2,478 (a Parkinson's drug), heroin coded as DIAMORPHINE 282, levomethadone 8, ethylmorphine 2, and benzhydrocodone 3. Benzhydrocodone (Apadaz) turns into hydrocodone in the body, but RxNorm files it as its own ingredient. open PV call: count it as hydrocodone or not?

Blank ingredient field: free text only (667 rows, 0.4%)
  • 471 distinct free-text names, e.g. TRAMADOL HCL ER, HYDROCODONE-ACETAMINOPH, OZEMPIC (2 MG/DOSE) SUBCUTANEOUS SO (cut off by the form).
  • RxNorm's best-guess search (approximateTerm, top 1) finds one of ours for 512 rows (77%). The walkthrough's word + brand rule finds 654 (98%). They agree on 507.
  • The best guess fails on cut-off and hyphenated text. The word rule fails on typos like ITRAMADOL HCL, which the best guess gets.
The surprise

On the 173,987 rows with prod_ai filled, RxNorm and the walkthrough's hand-written rule give the same answer on every row except 5 (the trihydrate case). The rule is not wrong today. RxNorm earns its place as the stable ID, the link to labels and packages, and a safe answer for strings nobody has seen yet.

6

Decision to propose

Proposed
  • Drug key = ingredient RxCUI (tty IN). The formulary stays an explicit list of 13 RxCUIs (D018).
  • One drug_identity table, keyed by the raw string: raw string → IN RxCUI, match method (exact / salt-stripped / best guess / word rule / none), RxNorm version used. Seed it with today's 41 strings plus the blank-field names. Look up only strings it has not seen.
  • Order of matching: RxNorm exact → RxNorm after salt strip (add "trihydrate") → for blank prod_ai, word + brand rule → approximateTerm. Never substring.
  • Unknown strings are tagged, not dropped silently: analogue / metabolite or unresolved. Monitoring counts new unresolved strings per quarter.
  • Combinations count for each of our ingredients in them and carry a combo tag with the partner list. Compounded mixes carry compounded.
  • Refresh the table monthly, after the RxNorm release. Use the API in the pilot; load the no-licence monthly zip for scale.

What moves forward

  • Into the build: the drug_identity table spec above and its seed (41 strings + 471 free-text names, from prototypes/data/_identity/cache/rxnav.json).
  • Into monitoring: share of our rows resolved per quarter (today 100% with the extended salt list), count of new unresolved strings, count of free-text-only rows (today 667).
  • Into the ontology: Drug object = 13 rows, one per IN RxCUI, with name, class (opioid / GLP-1) and RxNorm version.
  • Open questions: benzhydrocodone as hydrocodone (PV call, 3 rows) · do we show street analogues anywhere (43 rows)?
  • Your hand-check: in RxNav, look up OXYCODONE HYDROCHLORIDE TRIHYDRATE (expect no match) and DIAMORPHINE (expect heroin, 3304).
